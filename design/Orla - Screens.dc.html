How to read this document. One section per screen, and inside it the same screen at every width it was drawn for — 1440, then 1024, 768 and 390 — left to right, so a difference between breakpoints is a glance rather than a scroll. Pan sideways within a section; scroll down for the next screen. Error, loading and empty states follow the twelve product screens, each with its own breakpoints beside it.
768 & 390 — the desktop composition is the design. These are how it degrades: rails become drawers or inline cards, master–detail becomes navigation, and the primary action stays reachable as a sticky bar rather than being pushed below a scroll. Nothing sticky ever traps content — every scrolling pane carries bottom padding at least the height of its bar, so nothing can come to rest underneath one. Content passing beneath a translucent bar mid-scroll is intended; frames are frozen at a scroll position where a card boundary, never a price or a label, meets the bar's edge.
1024 — a real breakpoint, not compressed desktop. 1024×640 is a 13″ laptop's usable area once browser chrome is subtracted, and 640 of height is the binding constraint, not width. Sidebars keep their labels, right rails narrow but never stack, and grids lose a column before a card loses information.
Direction 1a, headline 4b. System rules held throughout: clay #B4552F is a fill only and clay-as-text is #A34A28; muted labels are #6B6459; app surfaces never scroll the page — panes scroll inside a fixed shell; colour is a signal, so clay means act here, sage means settled, gold means waiting on someone. Photography is labelled placeholder.
