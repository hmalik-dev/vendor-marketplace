Every state below is a real screen, not a fallback. Rules: one loading idiom per screen — skeletons for content, a 16px clay spinner for a single element, the wordmark pulse for first paint only, never two at once. Errors say what happened, whether money moved, and what to do next; a marketplace error that doesn't answer "was I charged?" is not finished. Empty states are the first impression of a feature, so each carries a serif headline and exactly one primary action. Colour keeps its meaning: clay = act here, gold = waiting on someone, steel = neutral information, red = something broke. Red is never used for "pending".

What I'd do next
Three things are stubbed rather than designed: the review submission flow, the packages editor, and the Stripe payout onboarding — all straightforward once these patterns are set. Note on public metrics: the landing page, sign-up panel and category cards carry no platform statistics — no vendor counts, no "events booked", no average rating. A new marketplace has none of those honestly, and a placeholder number is worse than none. Public pages prove themselves with mechanism instead (real availability, payment held until the event, no service fee). Metric-based marketing is deferred until the numbers are real and worth showing.
