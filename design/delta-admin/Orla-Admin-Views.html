OrlaAdmin views — delta to frame 13 and 22-admin.md
Three patterns, in the order they were asked for. A is a ruling — column grids, defaults and empty behaviour for four list routes that reuse frame 13's table verbatim. B is one drawn detail frame that settles card order, label/value typography, long-field wrapping and where destructive actions sit; the other three detail routes are given as card orders against it. C is the case detail, drawn in full, including the resolve control and its ConfirmAction.
One contradiction in the brief, resolved here. The nav is described as eight rows ending in Activity, but Cases is also said to carry a count badge — and Cases is not in the eight. A badge on a row that doesn't exist can't be drawn, so the rail is nine rows: Cases sits directly after Bookings, where the work arrives. Badges on Cases (open count) and Reviews (pending count) only. /admin/requests has no rail row either — it is a tab inside Bookings (Bookings · Requests), because a request is a booking before it exists and operators reach it while looking at bookings.
Colour is held to 40-states: steel information · gold waiting on someone · red failed · sage settled. So pending and quoted are gold, expired is stone (nothing failed — a clock ran out), declined and cancelled are stone, and red is reserved for payout failure, chargeback and refusal.

What this frame settles
Card order is question order. Money and platform state first (Stripe), then what the vendor sells, then what they show, then what their calendar owes. Identity is small and to the right because an operator arriving here already knows who they clicked.
Label/value typography. Labels 10.5px uppercase 600 stone-600 in a fixed 150px column; values 13px stone-900 in a minmax(0,1fr) column. Identifiers, dates and money are mono 12px — anything you'd paste into a support ticket looks pasteable.
Long fields wrap, never truncate. The value column is overflow-wrap:anywhere and grows the row. No ellipsis, no title-attribute tooltip: a truncated Stripe id is a call to support. Multi-part values (outstanding requirements) stack one per line under the label.
Destructive actions never sit inside read-only data. They live in one Actions card in the right column, below Identity, ordered least-to-most severe with a hairline between tiers, and each carries one line naming the consequence before it is pressed. Suspend is outlined red, never filled — a filled red button in a corner is a mis-click waiting.
Empty within a detail. A vendor with no packages keeps the card and the header count reads Packages · 0; the body is one stone-600 line — No packages yet. Customers can still request a custom quote. The card is never dropped, because a missing card reads as a loading bug.
The other three, as card orders
/admin/customers/[id]
Bookings → Reviews written · received (two tables, one card, tabbed) → Notifications sent. Notifications carry two status columns and they are not the same thing: Delivered/Bounced is the outcome, Read/Unread is the state. Right column: Identity, then Actions.
/admin/bookings/[id]
One card, not five: the money story reads top to bottom in event order — total → platform fee → vendor payout → paid_at → payout_released_at → payout attempts (each with its failure reason, red) → refund amount → cancellation reason → cancelled_by → dispute reason. Rows that don't apply are omitted, not dashed, so the card's length is the story's length. Money right-aligned mono, tabular, sub-totals indented.
/admin/users/[userId]
Retained data by category (count per category, table) → Legal acceptance (read-only: version, timestamp, IP). Right column actions: Export data then Close account. Close is drawn with its refusal in place — when a future confirmed booking exists the button is disabled and a gold panel above it reads Can't close: 1 confirmed booking on 12 Sep 2026. Cancel or complete it first. with a link to the booking. The 409 is shown as prevention, not as an error after the fact.

Why the control is drawn this way
Two positions, side by side, equal weight. Not a primary and a secondary — the operator's job is to judge, and a filled clay button on one side would be the product voting. Only the destructive edge is marked: outlined red, never filled red, until the confirm.
Each card names its consequence in money before it is pressed. Exact figures, the payout date by name, and what the other party gets — because the second thing an operator is asked afterwards is always "and what did the vendor get?"
The confirm restates, it doesn't summarise. Amount in the title, the counterparty's zero in the body, the field that will be written (cancelled_by = admin), and the one thing operators get wrong — that a refund doesn't close the bank's dispute. Cancel reads Keep the case open, naming the state you return to, not "Cancel", which in this screen is a verb about money.
Resolve is last on the page and inside a clay-edged card. The complaint and the evidence are above it; the region numbering (1 · 2 · 3) is visible so an operator who jumped can see what they skipped.
